Preparedness

Household preparedness

Last reviewed: 10 November 2026

Practical, information-only guidance for a family of four in Charleston, Dorchester, or Berkeley County, with a clear starting point for 72 hours, a two-week home supply, and a printable family plan.

This is general household information, not official emergency instructions. In an emergency, follow local officials, the National Weather Service, and call 911 if life is at risk.

Start here

  • Prepare for 72 hours
  • Build a two-week supply
  • Complete the family plan

For 3- and 14-day water and calorie totals, including pets, use the 3- and 14-day supply calculator.

This is a family reference, not a shop. It is written for a family of four in Charleston, Dorchester, or Berkeley County, South Carolina, where hurricanes, flooding, long power outages, and dangerous heat when the air conditioning fails are the usual risks. After a major event, it can take days for roads, water, power, and stores to work normally. Local emergency managers ask residents to manage on their own for at least the first 72 hours. Being able to stay at home for about two weeks, if you are not told to leave, covers far more real events.

Planning quantities for a family of four

Official U.S. guidance uses 1 gallon of water per person per day for drinking and sanitation. In very hot weather, Ready.gov notes that needs can double, so treat the gallon as a floor. For four people, that is 12 gallons for 3 days and 56 gallons for 14 days.

Purpose Water, 4 people Food at 2,000 kcal/person/day, 4 people
3 days (evacuation or first 72 hours) 12 gallons 24,000 calories
2 weeks at home 56 gallons 112,000 calories

Add extra water and food for pets. Keep about a 7-day supply of prescription medicines if you can, as the Red Cross recommends.

The food totals use a planning assumption of 2,000 calories per person per day. That is the general figure used on U.S. Nutrition Facts labels: “2,000 calories a day is used for general nutrition advice.” It is not a personal prescription. Needs vary by age, sex, height, weight, and activity; see the USDA ranges on the food page. Stock familiar meals first, then use the 2,000-calorie figure (or a personal USDA estimate) as a check. Use the 3- and 14-day supply calculator for 3- and 14-day water and calorie totals, including pets.

Keep medicines, documents, a headlamp, a charged power bank, and a small amount of cash where anyone in the house can find them in the dark.

How official information fits together

  • National Weather Service Charleston tells you what the weather is doing: watches, warnings, surge and flood risks, and tropical updates.
  • South Carolina Emergency Management Division and your county emergency management office tell you whether to evacuate, which zones are included, which routes to use, and when shelters are open.
  • Ready.gov, the CDC, the EPA, and the American Red Cross describe household supplies and the treatment and safety rules behind this section.

Always follow the instructions in an official alert or from local officials, even if they differ from a checklist on this site. If life is at risk, call 911.

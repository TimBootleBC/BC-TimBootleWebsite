Preparedness

Household preparedness

Practical, information-only guidance for a Charleston-area household of 4 to 6 people to get through the first days of an emergency, and to stay at home for up to two weeks if services are down.

This is general household information, not official emergency instructions. In an emergency, follow local officials, the National Weather Service, and call 911 if life is at risk.

This section is a family reference, not a shop. It is here so you can see what to have, how much of it a household of 4 to 6 people actually needs, and where official help and warnings come from in the Charleston area.

Disasters in the Lowcountry rarely announce themselves with much warning you can use at the last minute. Hurricanes are the obvious seasonal risk, from 1 June to 30 November, but flooding, long power outages, and dangerous heat when the air conditioning fails can arrive with a storm or on an ordinary summer week. After a major event, it can take days for roads, water, power, and stores to work normally again. Local emergency managers have long asked residents to be ready to manage on their own for at least the first 72 hours. That is the right starting point. It is even better to be able to stay at home for about two weeks if you are not told to leave.

Start with three decisions

  1. Know how you will hear official instructions. Sign up for county alerts, keep a NOAA weather radio, and understand Wireless Emergency Alerts on your phones. Details are on Communications and Charleston and South Carolina resources.
  2. Decide, in writing, how you will stay or go. Look up your evacuation zone by address, pick routes and a meeting place, and pack a go-bag. See The first 72 hours, Evacuation and go-bag, and the family plan.
  3. Stock the house, then the bag. Water, food, medicines, light, a way to charge a phone, and sanitation supplies at home cover most events. The bag is what you grab if you have to leave. Quantities for 4 to 6 people are on each supplies page.

A realistic baseline for 4 to 6 people

Official U.S. guidance uses 1 gallon of water per person per day for drinking and sanitation. In very hot weather, people may need more. For a household of 4 to 6:

Purpose Water Ready-to-eat food (planning figure)
3 days (evacuation or first 72 hours) 12–18 gallons Roughly 18,000–27,000 calories, or 3 days of meals your family will actually eat
2 weeks at home 56–84 gallons On the order of 84,000–126,000 calories (about 1,500 calories per person per day), plus infant or medical diets if you need them

Add extra water and food for pets. Keep a 7-day supply of prescription medicines if you can, and do not let the household’s only flashlight live at the back of a junk drawer.

These numbers are planning tools, not a shopping list for a particular brand. Use food you already eat, rotate it, and keep the urgent items — medicines, documents, a headlamp, a charged power bank, and a small amount of cash — where anyone in the house can find them in the dark.

How official information fits together

  • National Weather Service Charleston tells you what the weather is doing: watches, warnings, surge and flood risks, and tropical updates.
  • South Carolina Emergency Management Division and your county emergency management office tell you whether to evacuate, which zones are included, which routes to use, and when shelters are open.
  • Ready.gov and the American Red Cross describe the household supplies and family plans that work in most U.S. communities.

Always follow the instructions in an official alert or from local officials, even if they differ from a checklist on this site. If life is at risk, call 911.

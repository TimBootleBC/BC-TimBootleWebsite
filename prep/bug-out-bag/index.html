Bug-out bag checklist

Last reviewed: 10 November 2026

A printable 3-day go-bag checklist with suggested item weights, a running total of what you have checked, and a pack-weight limit you can set and edit.

This is general household information, not official emergency instructions. In an emergency, follow local officials, the National Weather Service, and call 911 if life is at risk.

This is a packing list for one adult 3-day go-bag, the kit described on Evacuation and go-bag. It is not the two-week home supply. Check off what you have packed, edit any weight to match the item in your hands, and set a pack-weight limit you can actually carry. Only checked items count toward the total. Your checks, weights, and limit are saved in this browser.

Water is the one precise default: the U.S. Geological Survey uses 8.34 pounds per U.S. gallon and 1 kilogram per liter. Three liters of water therefore weigh about 6.61 pounds (105.8 ounces), not counting the bottles. Every other default weight is a typical estimate. Weigh your own gear when you can.

Backpacking guides often suggest keeping a loaded pack at or below about 20 percent of body weight. REI states that guideline clearly and notes that it does not work in every situation. It is a hiking rule of thumb, not an official emergency-management limit. Set a number that fits the person who will carry the bag.

If officials tell you to leave, leave. Do not wait to finish packing. In a house fire, do not stop for the bag.

This checklist needs JavaScript in your browser to add up pack weight and to save your checks.

Leave a little room for last-minute items such as current medicines from the fridge or a child’s backpack. Pets need their own kit; the optional pet rows are a start, and the pets page has the fuller plan. A carrier is often carried separately from the backpack.

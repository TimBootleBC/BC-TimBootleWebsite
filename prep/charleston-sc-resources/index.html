Charleston and South Carolina resources

Official evacuation zones, alert systems, weather sources, and emergency contacts for households in and near Charleston, South Carolina.

This is general household information, not official emergency instructions. In an emergency, follow local officials, the National Weather Service, and call 911 if life is at risk.

This page collects official places to get warnings, look up your evacuation zone, and find help. Zone boundaries, routes, and phone numbers can change. Confirm them on the official sites, especially at the start of each hurricane season (1 June–30 November in the Atlantic).

In a life-threatening emergency, call 911. This page is a household reference, not a substitute for instructions from local officials or the National Weather Service.

Wireless Emergency Alerts on your phone and NOAA Weather Radio will carry many of the same official warnings without you having to watch a screen.

Know your evacuation zone

Storm surge is the main reason South Carolina uses coastal evacuation zones. The only reliable way to know your zone is to look up the address on SCEMD’s Know Your Zone map, or in the SC Emergency Manager app. Do not guess from a neighbor’s sign or from an older printed map. Zones were updated using newer surge models, and Berkeley and Dorchester Counties use different letter systems than Charleston County.

The summaries below are from the 2026 Charleston County Hurricane Guide and are here as a memory aid after you have looked up your address. They are not a substitute for the official map.

Charleston County (2026 guide)

  • Zone A: Areas in and around Edisto Island; the immediate coast including Seabrook Island, Kiawah Island, Folly Beach, Sullivan’s Island, Isle of Palms, and Dewees Island; and areas east of U.S. 17 south to Buck Hall Recreation Area.
  • Zone B: Areas north of Mount Pleasant between Zone A and U.S. 17; Mount Pleasant, James Island, and Johns Island; and the southern part of the Charleston Peninsula up to Shipyard Creek.
  • Zone C: Areas between Highway 17 and the Charleston County line; areas north of Zone B along the I-26 corridor to the county line; and areas in and around West Ashley, and behind Edisto, Johns, and James Islands to the county line.

If you live in Berkeley or Dorchester County, use Know Your Zone. Those counties’ 2026 plan annex uses other letters (for example, parts of Berkeley include zones described as B, D, E, and F). A Charleston County letter will not automatically apply across the county line.

An evacuation order may include some zones and not others, depending on the storm. Leave when your zone is called, or earlier if you will need extra time.

Evacuation routes (confirm at the time)

SCEMD publishes planned routing for Charleston-area communities on hurricane.sc/evacuate. In very general terms, many western and island communities are directed toward U.S. 17 and then inland (for example toward Walterboro, Aiken, and I-20); downtown and some James Island / Folly Beach traffic may use I-26 (sometimes with reversed lanes, which historically have started at a designated crossover such as Near Exit 197 / Nexton when they are activated); East Cooper traffic may use I-526 or U.S. 17 toward I-26, with some Isle of Palms traffic directed north and then inland toward Columbia. These patterns are planning descriptions only.

When an evacuation is underway, follow:

  • Law enforcement and official traffic-control devices
  • SCDOT 511
  • County and SCEMD alerts

Never drive around barricades, and never drive through floodwater.

How to get local alerts

  1. Wireless Emergency Alerts — already on most phones; do not turn them off.
  2. Charleston County CHSAlerts — the county’s opt-in mass-notification system (text, call, or email). Sign up through Charleston County Emergency Management or the official CHSAlerts link the county publishes (the system has been rebranded over time; use the county website, not a random third-party page). Include the address you want covered.
  3. City of Charleston and other municipalities may also list alert sign-ups: see Alerts and Notifications.
  4. SCEMD / SC Emergency Manager app — zone maps, a personal plan, and state push alerts: start at hurricane.sc.
  5. NOAA Weather Radio and NWS Charleston.
  6. FEMA app — NWS alerts for up to five locations.

If you live in Berkeley, Dorchester, Colleton, or Georgetown County, add that county’s notification system as well.

Weather details that matter here

NWS Charleston issues the coastal flood, flash flood, tropical, and severe-weather products for this area. A few local definitions are useful so you can read a warning correctly:

  • Tropical storm watch / warning: sustained winds of 39–73 mph possible or expected over land.
  • Hurricane watch / warning: sustained winds of 74 mph or more possible or expected over land.
  • Storm surge watch: life-threatening inundation possible, generally within 48 hours.
  • Storm surge warning: life-threatening inundation expected, generally within 36 hours (sometimes earlier if tropical-storm-force winds will cut off the time you have to leave).
  • Coastal flood products at this office are tied in part to the Charleston Harbor tide gage. As of the office’s published criteria, an advisory range starts around 7.0 feet MLLW, and watch/warning criteria at 8.0 feet MLLW at Charleston Harbor (tidal Berkeley and the Georgia coast use different numbers). These are office criteria, not a do-it-yourself forecast. Always read the actual product on weather.gov/chs.

County emergency management

Charleston County Emergency Management
8500 Palmetto Commerce Parkway, North Charleston, SC 29456
Phone: (843) 746-3800
Website: charlestoncounty.gov/departments/emergency-management

The office coordinates public warning, the Emergency Operations Center, Community Emergency Response Team (CERT) training, and preparedness outreach. Printed hurricane guides are typically updated each year; pick up a current copy and still verify zones online.

For neighboring counties, SCEMD’s Know Your Zone page links to Beaufort, Berkeley, Colleton, Dorchester, Georgetown, Horry, and Jasper emergency management offices.

Seasonal reminder

Atlantic hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November. Review zones, alerts, insurance, medicines, and the two-week water and food counts in May. A storm can still affect the area outside those dates, and flooding and long outages are not limited to hurricanes.

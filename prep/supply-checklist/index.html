Supply checklist

Last reviewed: 10 November 2026

A printable 3-day and 2-week emergency supply checklist for a Charleston-area household of 4 to 6 people, including water quantities and a food-planning method.

This is general household information, not official emergency instructions. In an emergency, follow local officials, the National Weather Service, and call 911 if life is at risk.

Household supply calculator

Planning figures only: 1 gallon of water and 2,000 calories per person per day. The calorie default matches the U.S. Nutrition Facts label figure (“2,000 calories a day is used for general nutrition advice”). It is not a personal prescription. Adjust using the USDA ranges on the food page.

Print this page and tick what you already have. Quantities are for a household of 4 to 6 unless a line says otherwise. Water uses the official 1 gallon per person per day planning rule from Ready.gov and the Red Cross. Food uses a planning assumption of 2,000 calories per person per day, the general figure on U.S. Nutrition Facts labels (FDA). Needs vary by age and activity; see the USDA ranges.

For the written family plan, see Family plan and checklists.

Add the same daily water allowance for each pet, and more in extreme heat (Ready.gov notes that needs can double).

Three-day (go-bag and first 72 hours)

  • Water in the amounts above, including some sealed bottles in the bags
  • Three days of familiar, ready-to-eat meals and snacks the household will actually eat
  • Manual can opener
  • Infant formula, baby food, and diapers if needed (ready-to-feed formula if water quality may be uncertain)
  • Pet food, water, leash or carrier, and medicines
  • Prescription medicines for at least 3 days, ideally 7 days, plus a written list of doses and allergies
  • Spare glasses or contact supplies; hearing-aid or device batteries
  • First-aid kit
  • Flashlight or headlamp for each person, plus spare batteries
  • Charged power bank for each person who relies on a phone (see capacities on Power and lighting), with the correct cables
  • Battery or hand-crank radio that can receive NOAA Weather Radio
  • Important numbers on paper; document pouch; some cash in small bills
  • Change of clothes, spare socks, rain gear, and sturdy shoes for each person
  • Hygiene items, wipes, soap or sanitizer, menstrual or incontinence supplies
  • Lightweight blankets or emergency blankets
  • Whistle, work gloves, and a dust mask or respirator
  • Comfort item and activities for children

Two weeks at home (in addition to the three-day layer)

  • Water in the 14-day amounts above, in original bottles or food-grade containers, dated
  • Fourteen days of familiar shelf-stable meals, rotated first-in, first-out
  • Optional check: daily calories in those meals compared with the 2,000-calorie planning figure and the USDA ranges for each person
  • Pet food and water for 14 days
  • Hygiene and sanitation supplies for 14 days (toilet bags, bucket with a lid, unscented bleach, soap, toilet paper, wipes)
  • Extra batteries; power banks kept on a charging routine
  • Generator fuel, if you have a generator, stored legally, with a written list of loads you will actually run
  • Blankets or sleeping bags for everyone; a heat and cooling plan
  • Fire extinguisher; smoke and carbon monoxide alarms tested
  • Plastic sheeting, tape, and a utility shutoff tool if appropriate

Twice-yearly review

  • Water dates and quantities still match the household size
  • Food dates; children’s clothes in the bags still fit; seasonal clothes swapped
  • Medicines and batteries in date; power banks charged
  • Documents and insurance numbers updated; some cash still in the pouch
  • Alert sign-ups still work; new phones included
  • Pet records and microchip registration current
  • Walk through the fire exits and the 5-/10-/30-minute evacuation list once
